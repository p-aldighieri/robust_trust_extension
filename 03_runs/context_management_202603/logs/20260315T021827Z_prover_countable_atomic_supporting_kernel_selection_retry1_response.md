## Verdict

The stronger selector-existence claim is **not proved** by the already-banked countable-atomic direct-route inputs, but I also do **not** have a sound full counterexample yet. What is settled is narrower:

* rowwise attainment at a reduced maximizer (w^*), hence existence of honest rowwise minimizing kernels (\beta) with (\operatorname{supp}\beta_i\subseteq \arg\min_j(m_i!\cdot w_j^*)); and
* failure of the **arbitrary-selector** route: an honest rowwise minimizer need not make every on-path coordinate Bayes-optimal.  

That is exactly the remaining Theorem 2 hinge, because robust rationalizability requires message-by-message Bayes optimality after every on-path message, not merely rowwise attainment. 

## What the existing obstruction does and does not show

The finite two-message example already banked shows:

* (w^*) can be a reduced maximizer,
* every stochastic kernel can be an honest rowwise minimizing kernel at that (w^*),
* one honest selector can fail Bayes optimality on path,
* another honest selector can succeed. 

So the finite example kills only
[
\text{rowwise attainment}+\text{arbitrary honest }\beta \Longrightarrow \text{on-path Bayes optimality},
]
not the stronger existence statement requested in this move. The surviving issue is genuinely a **kernel-selection** problem on the rowwise tie sets. 

## Exact crux lemma

Let
[
J_i:=\arg\min_j (m_i!\cdot w_j^*),
\qquad
a_j(\beta):=\alpha\tau_j m_j+(1-\alpha)\sum_i \tau_i\beta_{ij}m_i.
]
The desired selector-existence lemma is equivalent to:

> Find (\beta) with (\operatorname{supp}\beta_i\subseteq J_i) for every (i), such that
> [
> a_j(\beta)\in N_W(w_j^*) \quad \text{for every on-path } j,
> ]
> equivalently
> [
> w_j^*\in \arg\max_{w\in W} a_j(\beta)\cdot w .
> ]

That is the exact reduced-form statement of the missing Theorem 2 certification step. 

## Why the natural positive proof stalls

The tempting proof is:

1. take a supporting supergradient of the reduced objective (R) at (w^*);
2. decompose the row-inf part row by row;
3. read off an honest kernel (\beta);
4. conclude (a_j(\beta)\in N_W(w_j^*)) coordinatewise.

The stall is at step 2.

For the rowwise functional
[
\phi_i(w):=\inf_j (m_i!\cdot w_j),
]
rowwise attainment gives (J_i\neq\varnothing), but it does **not** rule out a countable near-minimizing tail outside (J_i). In the countable atomic product, a supporting functional for (\phi_i) at (w^*) may be generated by the **tail geometry** of values arbitrarily close to the minimum, rather than by a countably additive probability on the actual argmin set (J_i). Honest kernels, however, are forced to use only actual indices in (J_i). That is precisely the gap between “there exists a supporting hyperplane for (R)” and “there exists an honest rowwise minimizing kernel producing the right coordinatewise posteriors.”

So the missing step is not ordinary tie-breaking. It is a representation issue:

**Needed assumption / replacement lemma.**
Every supporting supergradient of the row-inf part of (R) at (w^*) can be represented by a **countably additive** honest kernel (\beta) concentrated on the actual argmin sets (J_i).

Under that statement, the selector-existence lemma would go through immediately. Without it, the countable tail can still be doing the real work.

## Concrete local obstruction template

Here is the smallest clean mechanism I can isolate, although I am **not** claiming it as a fully verified reduced maximizer yet.

Take a compact convex (W\subset \mathbb R^2) with two distinguished exposed boundary points
[
u_0=(1,0),\qquad v_0=(0,1),
]
and two countable boundary families
[
u_n\to u_0,\qquad v_n\to v_0,
]
such that each (u_n,v_n) is itself exposed by some belief (m_n), and such that for every “(u)-type” row the unique actual minimizer is (v_0) but the values at (v_n) decrease to the same minimum, while for every “(v)-type” row the unique actual minimizer is (u_0) but the values at (u_n) decrease to the same minimum.

Then the local geometry has two features simultaneously:

* every honest rowwise minimizing kernel is forced to send the (u)-rows to (v_0) and the (v)-rows to (u_0);
* but perturbing (u_0) or (v_0) need not move the corresponding row infima at first order, because the opposite tail ((u_n)) or ((v_n)) still keeps the same floor in the limit.

That is the exact “phantom support” phenomenon that can make the posterior generated by any honest kernel point in the wrong normal direction even though (w^*) remains locally protected by the tail. In other words: the honest kernel must charge the **actual** minimizer, while the reduced optimality may be certified by the **approaching** minimizers.

I can turn that template into a theorem only after one further step: either verify a full reduced maximizer with this geometry, or prove that such tail-supported certificates can always be replaced by honest countably additive ones. At the moment, neither step is banked.

## Bottom line for this move

The safe conclusion is:

* the stronger selector-existence lemma is **still open on the current branch**;
* the finite obstruction already cleared by the reviewer is too weak to refute it;
* the missing issue is exactly the countable-tail representation problem above, not ordinary tie-breaking.   

Suggested next local action: isolate and attack the countably-additive supporting-supergradient lemma for the row-inf part at a reduced maximizer; if it fails, upgrade the tail-support template above into a full reduced-game counterexample.
