\title{
  Extending Theorem~2 of \emph{Robust Trust} to infinite $M$ and $\Theta$:\\
  \large A three-tier theorem via the payoff-profile menu engine
}
\author{Pedro Aldighieri}
\date{8 May 2026}

\begin{document}

\begin{abstract}
This note reports a three-tier infinite-dimensional extension of
Theorem~2 (``Robustly Rationalizable Solution'') from Dworczak \&
Smolin's \emph{Robust Trust} (2026). The earlier
restricted-game-plus-Lusin-lift route, which required three
additional hypotheses (\textbf{A5-thick}, \textbf{A8c-attain},
\textbf{TRE-gen-Hall}) even for value optimality, has been replaced
by a finite-dimensional \emph{payoff-profile menu engine}. The
reformulation is strictly stronger in three respects. (i)
\textbf{Tier~1a} (existence of a value-optimal agent strategy plus
$\eps$-adversaries) holds under the paper's standing hypotheses
alone; A5-thick and A8c-attain are no longer needed. (ii)
\textbf{Tier~1b} (exact adversary attainment) is now isolated behind
a clean \emph{exact-contact} condition endogenous to the chosen
optimal labeling. (iii) \textbf{Tier~2} (full robust
rationalizability) replaces deterministic TRE-gen-Hall with a
set-valued \emph{menu-Hall} calibration, with the conclusion read
$q_\beta$-almost-everywhere in the sense made precise below. A
sharpness package (cone intersection lemma + no-free-dust theorem)
shows menu-Hall is genuinely needed inside the engine: the
obstruction is intrinsic multi-dimensional vector balance, uniform
across all support patterns, and impervious to null-message dust
under atomless~$\tau$. A separate classification result shows the
witness's trust region is not primitive, so the witness exhibits a
menu-engine artefact rather than a counterexample to the unrestricted
infinite Theorem~2. A Tier~1a tightness theorem at the fixed-label
level shows the $\eps$-adversary of Tier~1a is the genuine ceiling
whenever exact-contact fails on a $\tau$-positive source set, and a
companion cleanup theorem reduces the exact-contact hypothesis to
its substantive content (contact nonemptiness) by absorbing the
selector clause into Jankov--Von~Neumann descriptive-set machinery.
A further primitive strengthening shows that under atomless
adviser-signal support~$\tau$ alone, exact-contact holds for some
value-optimal labeling --- a $\tau$-null Cantor canvas inside
$\suppset(\tau)$ supports a continuous surjection onto the optimal
payoff menu, and repainting only this null set turns Tier~1b
unconditional on the natural continuous-signal primitive.
\end{abstract}

\section{The setting and the question}\label{sec:setting}

We use the notation of \emph{Robust Trust}. $\Om$ is finite with
full-support prior~$\mu_0$; $s\in\Delta(\Om)$ has state-conditional
law $\pi(\cdot\mid\omega)$ and unconditional law
$\tau = \sum_\omega \mu_0(\omega)\pi(\cdot\mid\omega)$;
$M = \suppset(\tau)$; $\theta\in\Th$ (compact metric) is drawn from
$f(\cdot\mid\omega)$, independently of $s$ conditional on $\omega$;
$A$ is compact metric; and $u(a,\omega,\theta)$ is bounded and
continuous in~$a$. Let $\Si$ denote the agent's measurable strategies
$\sigma:M\times\Th\to\Delta(A)$ and $B$ the misaligned adviser's
measurable kernels $\beta:M\to\Delta(M)$. With probability $\alpha$
the adviser is aligned and reports $s$ truthfully; with probability
$1-\alpha$ the adviser is misaligned.

The agent's robust objective is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:U}
  U(\sigma) \;=\; \alpha\,\E_{\mathrm{id},\sigma}[u] + (1-\alpha)\,\inf_{\beta\in B}\E_{\beta,\sigma}[u],
  \qquad U^* \;=\; \sup_{\sigma\in\Si} U(\sigma),
\end{equation}
and Definition~2 (\emph{robust rationalizability}) asks for
$(\sigma^*,\beta^*)$ such that, at every on-path message~$m$, the
agent's prescribed continuation is Bayes-optimal under
$P_{\beta^*}(\cdot\mid m)$. The paper's Theorem~2 establishes
existence under the assumption that $M$ and $\Th$ are \emph{finite},
via a finite Sion saddle. The question addressed here is whether
existence can be extended when $M$ and~$\Th$ are infinite.

\paragraph{The right reading of ``for all $m$'' in the infinite setting.}
Section~2 of \emph{Robust Trust} states that, in infinite spaces,
``statements involving `for all' should be interpreted as `for almost
all' with respect to the underlying distributions.'' Definition~2
itself does not name the underlying distribution. The natural choice
is the actual mixture message marginal under $\beta^*$,
\[
  q_{\beta^*} \;:=\; \alpha\,\tau \;+\; (1-\alpha)\!\int_M\!\beta^*(\cdot\mid s)\,\tau(ds),
\]
because $P_{\beta^*}(\cdot\mid m)$ is itself a regular conditional
probability defined $q_{\beta^*}$-a.e. Two consequences. First, the
right reading of Definition~2 in the infinite setting is
$q_{\beta^*}$-a.e., not literal-all and not merely $\tau$-a.e. Second,
the adversary is \emph{not} required to be $\tau$-absolutely
continuous: $\beta^*$ may place positive mass on a $\tau$-null Borel
set $N\subseteq M$, in which case $q_{\beta^*}(N)>0$ and those
messages are on-path for the mixture law. In the paper's finite
proof, $\alpha>0$ ensures every $m\in M$ has positive mixture mass,
so all three readings coincide; in infinite~$M$, $\tau$-null but
$q_{\beta^*}$-positive messages must satisfy the Bayes-optimality
condition if the adversary uses them.

\section{The engine}\label{sec:engine}

The earlier route worked in the agent's strategy space with the
Balder weak topology on $(\Si,T_\lambda)$, used Mertens's asymmetric
minmax theorem on the restricted game, and invoked Lusin
regularization to lift the restricted maximizer to the unrestricted
game. That machinery is heavy and required three added hypotheses,
the most consequential being A5-thick (a Lusin-thickness clause
imposed on a representative of the maximizer).

The current route works instead in the finite-dimensional
\emph{payoff-profile space}
\[
  W \;\subseteq\; \R^{|\Om|},
  \qquad
  W \;:=\; \big\{w\in\R^{|\Om|} : w(\omega) = \E_{\hat\sigma}[u(a,\omega,\theta)\mid\omega]\text{ for some }\hat\sigma\in\hat\Si\big\},
\]
where $\hat\Si$ is the space of private strategies $\hat\sigma:\Th\to\Delta(A)$.
$W$ is a compact convex subset of $\R^{|\Om|}$. The agent's optimization
reduces to choosing a Borel labeling $w:M\to W$ (a payoff-profile
strategy); the misaligned adviser optimizes over Borel kernels
$\beta:M\to\Delta(M)$; the value of the game depends on $w$ only
through the compact \emph{menu} $C := \overline{w(M)}\subseteq W$.

This finite-dimensional reformulation has three advantages. First,
the space $\mathcal K(W)$ of nonempty compact subsets of $W$ is
itself compact under the Hausdorff metric, so existence of an
optimal menu $C^*$ comes for free without any thickness or
representative-choice hypothesis. Second, a closure-pruning step
cleanly identifies the support $C^\dagger := \overline{w^*(M)}\subseteq C^*$
of an optimal labeling, decoupling existence from contact-set
geometry. Third, the rowwise contact set
\[
  G(s) \;:=\; \{m\in M : s\cdot w^*(m) = \min_{z\in C^\dagger} s\cdot z\}
\]
becomes a finite-dimensional object whose existence and selectability
properties are easy to state and easy to fail in tight examples.

\section{Main theorem}\label{sec:main}

The standing hypotheses of \emph{Robust Trust} are kept verbatim
($\Om$ finite, $\mu_0$ full support, $A,\Th$ compact metric, $u$
bounded continuous in $a$, conditional independence of $s,\theta$
given~$\omega$, all measurability Borel). The new theorem has three
tiers, with the second and third each layering one additional
endogenous condition.

\paragraph{Tier 1a (standing hypotheses alone).}
There exists $\sigma^*\in\Si$ with $U(\sigma^*) = U^*$. For every
$\eps>0$, there exists $\beta_\eps\in B$ with
$U(\beta_\eps,\sigma^*) \le U^* + \eps$.

\paragraph{Exact-contact condition.}
With $w^*$ the chosen optimal labeling and $C^\dagger := \overline{w^*(M)}$,
the rowwise contact set $G(s)$ is nonempty for $\tau$-a.e.~$s$ and
$s\mapsto G(s)$ admits a Borel measurable selector
$m^*:M\to M$ with $m^*(s)\in G(s)$ $\tau$-a.e.

\paragraph{Tier 1b (+ exact-contact).}
With $\beta^*(dm\mid s) := \delta_{m^*(s)}(dm)$,
$U(\beta^*,\sigma^*) = \inf_{\beta\in B} U(\beta,\sigma^*) = U^*$.

\paragraph{Menu-Hall condition.}
There exists a set-valued kernel $\kappa:M\to\Delta(M)$ supported
on $G(s)$ (i.e.\ $\kappa(G(s)\mid s) = 1$ for $\tau$-a.e.~$s$) such
that, for the joint coupling
$\gamma_\alpha := \alpha\,(\mathrm{id},\mathrm{id})_\#\tau + (1-\alpha)\,\tau\otimes\kappa$
on $M\times M$ with second-marginal $q := (\gamma_\alpha)_2$, the
posterior satisfies $P_{\gamma_\alpha}(\cdot\mid m)\in B(m)$ for
$q$-a.e.~$m$, where
$B(m) := \{\mu\in\Delta(\Om) : \hat\sigma^*(m)\in\argmax_{\hat\sigma'} U(\hat\sigma',\mu)\}$
is the Bayes cone for the agent's continuation at message~$m$.

\paragraph{Tier 2 (+ menu-Hall).}
With $\beta^* := \kappa$,
$\hat\sigma^*(m)\in\argmax_{\hat\sigma'}U(\hat\sigma',P_{\gamma_\alpha}(\cdot\mid m))$
for $q$-a.e.~$m$. Hence $\sigma^*$ is robustly rationalizable in
the paper's a.e./on-path sense. When $\alpha>0$, $q\ge\alpha\tau$,
so the conclusion also holds $\tau$-a.e.

\section{Proof strategy in one paragraph}\label{sec:strategy}

\textbf{Tier 1a.} The value of the game depends on $w$ only through
the menu $C$, and the menu-value functional
$F(C) := \int_M[\alpha\max_{w\in C} m\cdot w + (1-\alpha)\min_{w\in C} s\cdot w]\,\tau(ds)$
is upper semicontinuous on the Hausdorff-compact $\mathcal K(W)$.
Hence an optimal $C^*$ exists, an optimal labeling
$w^*:M\to C^*$ realizing $\max_{w\in C^*} m\cdot w$ pointwise exists
by Aliprantis--Border 18.19, and a profile-realization sub-lemma
(KRN selection on $\hat\Si\twoheadrightarrow W$) lifts $w^*$ to a
private strategy $\hat\sigma^*$. The closure-pruning step
$C^\dagger := \overline{w^*(M)}$ preserves the value because
$\min_{w\in C^\dagger}s\cdot w \ge \min_{w\in C^*}s\cdot w$ pointwise
and $C^*$ was already optimal. The $\eps$-adversary is built by
Jankov--von Neumann selection on the $\eps$-thick approximate
contact set $G_\eps(s) := \{m : s\cdot w^*(m)\le \min_{C^\dagger}s\cdot w + \eps\}$.

\textbf{Tier 1b.} Under exact-contact, replace $G_\eps$ by $G$
and the selector $m^*$ delivers $\beta^*(\cdot\mid s) := \delta_{m^*(s)}$
exactly attaining the rowwise minimum $\tau$-a.e.

\textbf{Tier 2.} Under menu-Hall, the support-function form
$\int_E\phi(P_{\gamma_\alpha}(\cdot\mid m))q(dm) \le \int_E h_{B(m)}(\phi)q(dm)$
for every measurable $E$ and continuous affine $\phi$ is equivalent
(by countable separation in finite-dim $\Delta(\Om)$) to pointwise
membership $P_{\gamma_\alpha}(\cdot\mid m)\in B(m)$ $q$-a.e., which
is exactly the Bayes-optimality requirement of Definition~2. Since
$q\ge\alpha\tau$ when $\alpha>0$, $q$-a.e.~upgrades to $\tau$-a.e.

\section{Tier 1a: from menu existence to $\eps$-adversaries}\label{sec:tier1a}

\begin{lemma}[L1 --- menu-value equivalence]\label{lem:menu-value}
For any Borel labeling $w:M\to W$ with menu $C := \overline{w(M)}$,
\[
  U(\sigma_w) \;=\; \alpha\!\int_M m\cdot w(m)\,\tau(dm) + (1-\alpha)\!\int_M\min_{z\in C} s\cdot z\,\tau(ds).
\]
The first term equals $\int_M\max_{z\in C}m\cdot z\,\tau(dm)$ at any
labeling that selects pointwise maximizers on $C$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}[Sketch]
The aligned term integrates the agent's payoff at the truthful
posterior $m$ against the chosen profile $w(m)$; the misaligned
term integrates the rowwise infimum over messages, which equals the
infimum over $w(M)$ of $s\cdot w$, which by continuity equals the
minimum over $\overline{w(M)}$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[L2 --- existence of an optimal menu]\label{lem:menu-exist}
There exists $C^*\in\mathcal K(W)$ maximizing
$F(C) := \int[\alpha\max_{w\in C}m\cdot w + (1-\alpha)\min_{w\in C}s\cdot w]\tau(ds)$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}[Sketch]
$\mathcal K(W)$ is compact under Hausdorff distance because $W$ is
compact in $\R^{|\Om|}$. The aligned term is continuous in $C$
(maximum of a continuous function over a Hausdorff-continuous compact
set), and the misaligned term is upper semicontinuous in $C$
(minimum is u.s.c.\ in $C$). Hence $F$ is u.s.c., and
$\mathcal K(W)$-compactness gives a maximizer.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[L3 --- closure-pruning]\label{lem:pruning}
Let $w^*:M\to C^*$ be an optimal Borel labeling and
$C^\dagger := \overline{w^*(M)}$. Then $F(C^\dagger) = F(C^*) = U^*$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}[Sketch]
$w^*(m)\in C^\dagger\subseteq C^*$, so the aligned term is unchanged
pointwise. $C^\dagger\subseteq C^*$ implies
$\min_{C^\dagger}s\cdot w \ge \min_{C^*}s\cdot w$, so the misaligned
term weakly improves. Optimality of $C^*$ forces equality.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[L4 --- $\eps$-adversary realization]\label{lem:eps-adv}
For every $\eps>0$, define
$G_\eps(s) := \{m\in M : s\cdot w^*(m)\le \min_{z\in C^\dagger}s\cdot z + \eps\}$.
Then $s\mapsto G_\eps(s)$ has analytic graph and admits a universally
measurable selector $m_\eps^*$. The kernel
$\beta_\eps(\cdot\mid s) := \delta_{m_\eps^*(s)}$ satisfies
$U(\beta_\eps,\sigma^*) \le U^* + (1-\alpha)\eps$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}[Sketch]
$G_\eps(s)$ is the preimage under $m\mapsto s\cdot w^*(m)$ of an
interval of width $\eps$ shifted by an essentially-bounded measurable
function $s\mapsto\min_{C^\dagger}s\cdot w$; its graph is analytic.
Jankov--von Neumann \cite[Thm.~18.13]{AliprantisBorder2006} provides
a universally measurable selector. The payoff bound follows by
plugging $\beta_\eps$ into the misaligned term of $U$.
\end{proof}

Tier 1a follows: existence of $\sigma^*$ by Lemmas~\ref{lem:menu-exist}
and \ref{lem:pruning} plus the profile-realization sub-lemma; the
$\eps$-adversary by Lemma~\ref{lem:eps-adv}.

\section{Tier 1b: exact-contact}\label{sec:tier1b}

The exact-contact condition isolates the gap between
$\eps$-attainment and exact attainment. There are several
sufficient routes, all standard:

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=2em]
\item \textbf{Closed image.} If $w^*(M)\subseteq W$ is closed, then
$C^\dagger = w^*(M)$, the rowwise minimum on $C^\dagger$ is attained
on $w^*(M)$, and any minimizer pulls back to $G(s)\ne\emptyset$.
\item \textbf{Closed-graph correspondence.} If
$\Gamma(s) := \argmin_{w\in C^\dagger}s\cdot w$ is upper hemicontinuous
with closed values, the measurable maximum theorem
\cite[Thm.~18.19]{AliprantisBorder2006} delivers a Borel measurable
selector.
\item \textbf{u.h.c.\ Bayes-action.} If the agent's Bayes-action
correspondence at message $m$ is u.h.c.\ with closed compact values,
then choosing $\hat\sigma^*$ as a continuous selector yields a closed
labeling, hence closed image.
\end{itemize}

Under exact-contact, Tier 1b is immediate: $\beta^*(\cdot\mid s) := \delta_{m^*(s)}$
attains $\min_{w\in C^\dagger}s\cdot w$ pointwise $\tau$-a.e., so
$U(\beta^*,\sigma^*) = U^*$.

\section{Tier 1a tightness and Tier 1b cleanup}\label{sec:tightness}

Two structural facts complete the Tier~1a/1b picture at the fixed
optimal $\sigma^*$. Theorem~\ref{thm:tightness} shows that the
$\eps$-adversary of Lemma~\ref{lem:eps-adv} is the genuine ceiling
whenever the rowwise contact set $G(s)$ is empty on a $\tau$-positive
source set; Theorem~\ref{thm:cleanup} shows that the ``Borel
selector'' clause of the exact-contact condition (\S\ref{sec:tier1b})
is automatic once contact nonemptiness is granted. Together they
classify exact-contact as a single, irreducible bridge between
Tier~1a and Tier~1b (Corollary~\ref{cor:bridge}).

Throughout, write $h(s,m) := s\cdot w^*(m)$ and
$r(s) := \min_{z\in C^\dagger} s\cdot z = \inf_{m\in M} h(s,m)$ (last
equality by Lemma~\ref{lem:pruning}); $h$ is bounded Borel, $r$ is
continuous (lower envelope of a compact family of linear forms over
compact $C^\dagger$).

\begin{theorem}[Fixed-label tightness]\label{thm:tightness}
Let $E := \{s\in M : G(s) = \emptyset\}$. The set $E$ is coanalytic,
hence universally measurable; suppose $\tau(E)>0$ in the
$\tau$-completion. Then:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=2em,label=(\roman*)]
\item For every $\eps>0$ there is $\beta_\eps\in B$ with
$\int\!\int h(s,m)\,\beta_\eps(dm\mid s)\,\tau(ds) \le \int r\,d\tau + \eps$.
\item No $\beta\in B$ satisfies
$\int\!\int h(s,m)\,\beta(dm\mid s)\,\tau(ds) = \int r\,d\tau$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The graph
$\Gamma := \{(s,m)\in M\times M : h(s,m) = r(s)\}$ is Borel (zero
set of the bounded Borel function $f(s,m) := h(s,m) - r(s)$ on the
standard Borel space $M\times M$). Its $s$-projection
$E^c = \pi_M(\Gamma)$ is analytic; $E$ is coanalytic; both are
universally measurable.

\emph{(ii).} Suppose some $\beta\in B$ attains the infimum. With
$\nu(ds,dm) := \tau(ds)\,\beta(dm\mid s)$,
$0 = \int f\,d\nu$ and $f\ge 0$ Borel, so $f = 0$ $\nu$-a.e., i.e.\
$\int_M \beta(G(s)^c\mid s)\,\tau(ds) = 0$. The integrand is
nonnegative Borel, so $\beta(G(s)\mid s) = 1$ for $\tau$-a.e.\ $s$.
On $E$ this forces $\beta(\emptyset\mid s) = 1$, contradicting
$\tau(E) > 0$.

\emph{(i).} $G_\eps := \{f\le\eps\}$ is Borel with nonempty
sections by definition of $\inf$. $M$ compact metric is standard
Borel, so Jankov--Von~Neumann
\cite[Thm.~18.13]{AliprantisBorder2006} yields a universally
measurable selector $m_\eps^u$. Since $\tau$ is a probability on a
standard Borel space, $m_\eps^u$ admits a Borel modification
$\tilde m_\eps$ equal $\tau$-a.e.\ (see \cite{Bogachev2007}). Set
$\beta_\eps(\cdot\mid s) := \delta_{\tilde m_\eps(s)}$.
Then $\int h(s,\tilde m_\eps(s))\,\tau(ds)\le \int(r(s)+\eps)\,\tau(ds)
= \int r\,d\tau + \eps$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Dust does not repair the obstruction]
The $q_\beta$-a.e.\ reading of Definition~2 (\S\ref{sec:setting})
allows $\beta$ to place positive mass on $\tau$-null messages, which
affects the per-message Bayes-optimality requirement but \emph{not}
the rowwise equality $h(s,m) = r(s)$ used here. Routing $\beta$-mass
to a $\tau$-null dust point cannot create a rowwise minimizer for a
source $s\in E$ if no message in $M$ satisfies the equality. Hence
Theorem~\ref{thm:tightness} is robust to the $q_\beta$-a.e.\ reading.
\end{remark}

\begin{theorem}[Selector is automatic]\label{thm:cleanup}
Under the standing hypotheses, with $M$ standard Borel and $w^*$
Borel, the following are equivalent:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=2em,label=(\alph*)]
\item (exact-contact, \S\ref{sec:tier1b}) $G(s)\ne\emptyset$
$\tau$-a.e., and there exists a Borel selector $m^*:M\to M$ with
$m^*(s)\in G(s)$ $\tau$-a.e.;
\item (cleanup) $G(s)\ne\emptyset$ $\tau$-a.e.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Sketch]
(a)$\Rightarrow$(b) trivial. (b)$\Rightarrow$(a): $\Gamma$ is Borel
with $\tau$-a.e.\ nonempty sections; Jankov--Von~Neumann gives a
universally measurable selector on $P := \{s : G(s)\ne\emptyset\}$;
the standard $\tau$-a.e.\ Borel-modification lemma on a standard
Borel space supplies a Borel selector. Extend by any Borel default
on $M\setminus P$ (a $\tau$-null set).
\end{proof}

Theorem~\ref{thm:cleanup} reduces the three sufficient routes of
\S\ref{sec:tier1b} (closed image, closed-graph correspondence, u.h.c.\
Bayes-action) to a single substantive content: each acts through
contact nonemptiness, while the ``Borel selector'' half is
descriptive-set machinery rather than a primitive structural
hypothesis.

\begin{corollary}[The bridge is one condition]\label{cor:bridge}
For the chosen optimal $\sigma^*$, with $w^*$ Borel and $M$ standard
Borel,
\[
  \exists\,\beta^*\in B:\
  \int\!\!\int h(s,m)\,\beta^*(dm\mid s)\,\tau(ds) = \int r\,d\tau
  \;\Longleftrightarrow\;
  G(s)\ne\emptyset \text{ for $\tau$-a.e.\ }s.
\]
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}[Sketch]
($\Rightarrow$) Theorem~\ref{thm:tightness}\,(ii) contrapositive.
($\Leftarrow$) Theorem~\ref{thm:cleanup} provides a Borel
$m^*(s)\in G(s)$ $\tau$-a.e.; then
$\beta^*(\cdot\mid s) := \delta_{m^*(s)}$ attains the infimum.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{What this does \emph{not} establish.}
Theorem~\ref{thm:tightness} is fixed-label: it concerns a specific
optimal $\sigma^*$ and its labeling $w^*$. A \emph{primitive}
obstruction --- showing that for some primitive
$(\Om,\mu_0,\tau,A,\Th,u,\alpha)$ \emph{every} value-optimal
$\sigma^*$ has $\tau$-positive contact failure --- is not proved
here and remains the right open question for certifying Tier~1a's
ceiling as primitively sharp. Tier~1a's value-side conclusion and
Tier~2's menu-Hall requirement are unaffected. The
deletion-compatible Hall duality theorem flagged in the project
record remains the right open question for Tier~2.

\paragraph{What \emph{not} to substitute for exact-contact.}
$\tau$-a.e.\ continuity of $w^*$ is the wrong regularity notion: a
single $\tau$-null discontinuity can be the rowwise minimizer for a
$\tau$-positive source set, and the adversary may carry positive
$q_\beta$-mass to it. Strict concavity in the agent's action plus
a.e.\ unique-argmax hypotheses do not, by themselves, bypass
exact-contact; only $w^*$ continuous on all of~$M$ (not just
$\tau$-a.e.) reduces exact-contact to a free consequence.

\section{Tier 1b strengthening: unconditional under atomless~$\tau$}\label{sec:tier1b-atomless}

\S\ref{sec:tier1b} and \S\ref{sec:tightness} characterised exact-contact as
the irreducible bridge between Tier~1a's $\eps$-ceiling and Tier~1b's
exact attainment, for a fixed optimal labeling $w^*$. This section
delivers a \emph{primitive} strengthening: under the standing
hypotheses augmented by atomlessness of $\tau$ alone, exact-contact
holds for \emph{some} value-optimal labeling, and Tier~1b becomes
unconditional.

\begin{theorem}[Tier 1b under atomless $\tau$]\label{thm:atomless-tier1b}
Assume the standing hypotheses of \S\ref{sec:setting} and $\tau$
atomless on $M = \suppset(\tau)$.
Then there exists a value-optimal agent strategy $\sigma^*$ whose
payoff-profile labeling $w^* : M \to W$ satisfies $G(s) \ne \emptyset$
for every $s \in M$. Consequently, by Theorem~\ref{thm:cleanup}, an
exact adversary $\beta^* \in B$ exists with $U(\beta^*, \sigma^*) = U^*$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Apply Lemmas~\ref{lem:menu-value}--\ref{lem:eps-adv} to obtain an
optimal Borel labeling $w_0 : M \to W$ with closure-pruned support
$C^\dagger_0 := \overline{w_0(M)}$ compact in $W$, $F(C^\dagger_0) = U^*$.

\emph{Null Cantor subset lemma.} Because $\tau$ is atomless and
$M = \suppset(\tau)$, $M$ contains no isolated points. We construct $N$
by a Cantor scheme. Choose two distinct points $m^0, m^1 \in M$; by
atomlessness and inner-regularity of $\tau$ on the Polish space $M$
\cite[Thm.~7.1.7, Vol.~II]{Bogachev2007}, pick disjoint compact
neighborhoods $F^0, F^1$ of $m^0, m^1$ with diameters $<1/2$ and
$\tau(F^0 \cup F^1) < 1/2$. Iterate: given $F^t$ for $t \in \{0,1\}^n$,
since $F^t$ has no isolated points and $\tau$ is atomless, pick two
distinct interior points $m^{t0}, m^{t1}$ and disjoint compact
neighborhoods $F^{t0}, F^{t1} \subseteq \mathrm{int}(F^t)$ with
diameters $<2^{-(n+1)}$ and
$\tau(F^{t0} \cup F^{t1}) < 2^{-(n+1)}$. Set
\[
  N := \bigcap_{n \ge 0} \bigcup_{t \in \{0,1\}^n} F^t.
\]
The shrinking-diameter compact-nested intersection gives a homeomorphism
$2^\omega \to N$ by $t \mapsto \bigcap_n F^{t|_n}$; $N$ is compact,
zero-dimensional, has no isolated points, $\tau(N) = 0$. See
\cite[Thm.~6.2]{Kechris1995} for the general Cantor-scheme embedding
of $2^\omega$ into a nonempty perfect Polish space.

\emph{Hausdorff--Alexandroff surjection.} Every compact metric space
is a continuous image of $2^\omega$
\cite[Thm.~4.18]{Kechris1995}. Composing with the homeomorphism
$N \cong 2^\omega$ yields a continuous (hence Borel) surjection
$\psi : N \to C^\dagger_0$.

\emph{Modified labeling.} Define
\[
  w^*(m) :=
  \begin{cases}
    \psi(m) & m \in N,\\
    w_0(m) & m \in M \setminus N.
  \end{cases}
\]
$w^*$ is Borel as a piecewise Borel function on the Borel partition
$(N, M \setminus N)$, equals $w_0$ $\tau$-a.e., and has image
$w^*(M) = \psi(N) \cup w_0(M \setminus N) = C^\dagger_0 \cup
w_0(M \setminus N) = C^\dagger_0$ (because $w_0(M) \subseteq C^\dagger_0$
and $\psi$ surjects). Hence $w^*(M) = C^\dagger_0$ is compact.

\emph{Profile-realization, global form.} The profile-realization map
$\Phi : \hat\Sigma \to W$ (continuous on the compact private-strategy
space $\hat\Sigma$, with $W = \Phi(\hat\Sigma)$ and nonempty compact
fibers; see~\S\ref{sec:engine}) admits a Borel right inverse
$R : W \to \hat\Sigma$ by Kuratowski--Ryll-Nardzewski measurable
selection \cite[Thm.~18.13]{AliprantisBorder2006}. Define the modified
agent strategy by the \emph{global} composition
\[
  \hat\sigma^*(m) := R(w^*(m)), \qquad
  \sigma^*(m, \theta) := \hat\sigma^*(m)(\theta) \quad \text{for all } m \in M.
\]
$\hat\sigma^*$ is Borel as the composition of Borel maps. Joint Borel
measurability of $(m,\theta) \mapsto \sigma^*(m,\theta) =
\hat\sigma^*(m)(\theta)$ follows because the evaluation map
$\mathrm{ev} : \hat\Sigma \times \Theta \to \Delta(A)$,
$(\hat\sigma, \theta) \mapsto \hat\sigma(\theta)$, is Borel on the
admissible strategy space of \S\ref{sec:engine}. By construction, the
induced payoff profile is $\Phi(R(w^*(m))) = w^*(m)$. The global form
bypasses any seam-measurability concern at $\partial N$.

\emph{Value preservation.} Since $w^* = w_0$ on $M \setminus N$ and
$\tau(N) = 0$, the aligned integral
$\alpha\!\int_M m \cdot w^*(m)\,\tau(dm) = \alpha\!\int_M m \cdot w_0(m)\,\tau(dm)$.
The misaligned term depends on $w$ only through the closure of its
image (Lemma~\ref{lem:menu-value}); $\overline{w^*(M)} = C^\dagger_0 =
\overline{w_0(M)}$, so the misaligned integral is unchanged. Hence
$U(\sigma^*) = U(\sigma_0) = U^*$.

\emph{Exact contact for every $s$, with measurable selector inside $N$.}
For each $s \in M$, the rowwise infimum
$r(s) = \min_{z \in C^\dagger_0} s \cdot z$ is attained on the compact
$C^\dagger_0$. Consider the correspondence
\[
  H : M \rightrightarrows N, \qquad
  H(s) := \{m \in N : \psi(m) \in \argmin_{z \in C^\dagger_0} s \cdot z\}.
\]
$H$ has nonempty compact values: nonempty because $\psi$ surjects onto
$C^\dagger_0$ and the rowwise minimum is attained; compact because
$\{m \in N : \psi(m) \in K\}$ is the preimage under the continuous
$\psi : N \to C^\dagger_0$ of a closed subset $K \subseteq C^\dagger_0$,
hence closed in the compact $N$. $H$ has Borel (in fact closed) graph
because the defining condition $s \cdot \psi(m) = r(s)$ is continuous
in $(s,m)$. By Kuratowski--Ryll-Nardzewski
\cite[Thm.~18.13]{AliprantisBorder2006}, $H$ admits a Borel selector
$m^* : M \to N$ with $m^*(s) \in H(s)$ for \emph{every}~$s$
(not merely $\tau$-a.e.). Set
$\beta^*(\cdot \mid s) := \delta_{m^*(s)}$; the map
$m \mapsto \delta_m$ is continuous from $M$ into $\Delta(M)$ with the
weak topology, so $\beta^* \in B$. For every $s$,
$s \cdot w^*(m^*(s)) = s \cdot \psi(m^*(s)) = r(s)$. Therefore
\begin{align*}
  U(\beta^*, \sigma^*)
    &= \alpha\!\int_M m \cdot w^*(m)\,\tau(dm)
       + (1-\alpha)\!\int_M s \cdot w^*(m^*(s))\,\tau(ds)\\
    &= \alpha\!\int_M m \cdot w^*(m)\,\tau(dm)
       + (1-\alpha)\!\int_M r(s)\,\tau(ds) \;=\; F(C^\dagger_0) \;=\; U^*
\end{align*}
by Lemma~\ref{lem:menu-value}. In particular $G(s) \ne \emptyset$ for
every $s$, and exact-contact holds in the form of \S\ref{sec:tier1b}.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Atomlessness is a primitive canvas condition]
Atomlessness of $\tau$ is a primitive structural hypothesis on the
adviser-signal distribution. It is \emph{not} logically comparable to
exact-contact (which is an endogenous property of a chosen labeling):
atomlessness is a sufficient \emph{primitive} canvas condition under
which a value-preserving null modification of any optimal labeling
yields an exact-contact representative. The minimal technical add-on
is the existence of a $\tau$-null Borel $N \subseteq M$ admitting a
Borel surjection onto $C^\dagger_0$; on compact metric standard Borel
$M$, atomlessness is the cleanest primitive guaranteeing this.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Atomless $\tau$ as economic primitive]
The unconditional finite-$M$ case in
\cite{DworczakSmolin2026} relies on $|M|<\infty$ and $\alpha>0$ to
make every message $q_\beta$-on-path; this is at the opposite end of
the distributional spectrum from atomless $\tau$. In the infinite-$M$
setting, atomlessness corresponds to continuously distributed adviser
posteriors --- the natural primitive when $\Theta$ is a continuum or
when the adviser observes a continuous signal, including the
binary-state continuous-density setting and the spherical example
worked in \cite{DworczakSmolin2026}. Theorem~\ref{thm:atomless-tier1b}
is therefore a clean continuous-signal add-on, not a hidden D\&S
primitive.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Tier 2 is \emph{not} addressed]
Theorem~\ref{thm:atomless-tier1b} does not provide menu-Hall. The
no-free-dust theorem (\S\ref{sec:tight}) rules out repairing a
menu-engine calibration failure by routing $q_\beta$-mass through
$\tau$-null messages; that obstruction is orthogonal to exact-contact.
Tier~2 still requires the menu-Hall calibration of \S\ref{sec:tier2}
or its sufficient instantiations.
\end{remark}

\section{Tier 2: menu-Hall calibration}\label{sec:tier2}

The menu-Hall condition asks that the rowwise minimization can be
implemented by a kernel $\kappa(\cdot\mid s)$ whose induced posterior
at the messages is Bayes-rationalizable for the agent's continuation
$\hat\sigma^*(m)$. Two equivalent forms:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=2em]
\item \textbf{Kernel form.} $\kappa(G(s)\mid s) = 1$ $\tau$-a.e., and
$P_{\gamma_\alpha}(\cdot\mid m)\in B(m)$ $q$-a.e.\ where
$\gamma_\alpha := \alpha(\mathrm{id},\mathrm{id})_\#\tau + (1-\alpha)\tau\otimes\kappa$
and $q := (\gamma_\alpha)_2$.
\item \textbf{Support-function form.} For every measurable $E\subseteq M$
and every continuous affine $\phi:\Delta(\Om)\to\R$,
\[
  \alpha\!\int_E\phi(m)\,\tau(dm) + (1-\alpha)\!\int_M\!\!\int_E\phi(s)\,\kappa(dm\mid s)\,\tau(ds) \;\le\; \int_E h_{B(m)}(\phi)\,q(dm),
\]
where $h_{B(m)}(\phi) := \sup_{\mu\in B(m)}\phi(\mu)$.
\end{itemize}
The support-function inequality, taken over a countable separating
family of affine $\phi$ and all measurable~$E$, is equivalent to the
pointwise membership statement $P_{\gamma_\alpha}(\cdot\mid m)\in B(m)$
$q$-a.e.; equivalence uses finite-dimensional separation in
$\Delta(\Om)$.

This is exactly the per-message Bayes-optimality requirement of
Definition~2. Tier 2 follows.

\section{Sharpness of menu-Hall}\label{sec:tight}

Menu-Hall is genuinely needed inside the engine. The witness is
ternary winner-takes-all: $\Om = \{0,1,2\}$, $A = \{a_0,a_1,a_2\}$
with $u(a_\omega,\omega) = 1$ and $u(a_i,\omega) = -1$ for
$i\ne\omega$, prior $\mu_0 = (1/3,1/3,1/3)$, atomless full-support
$\tau$ on $\Delta(\Om)$, and trust region
$T := \{\mu : \mu(0)\le 0.4\}$. The induced payoff-profile menu under
any plurality continuation is the full vertex set $C^\dagger = \{v_0,
v_1, v_2\}$ with $v_\omega(\omega) = 1$ and $v_\omega(\omega') = -1$
for $\omega'\ne\omega$.

For each nonempty $I \subseteq \{0,1,2\}$, define the rowwise
minimizer cone and Bayes-optimality cone of the mixed profile
$w_\lambda := \sum_{i\in I}\lambda_i v_i$ (with $\mathrm{supp}\,\lambda = I$):
\[
  K_I^- \;:=\; \{s\in\Delta(\Om) : s_i\le s_k\,\,\forall i\in I,\,\forall k\in\{0,1,2\}\},
\]
\[
  B_I \;:=\; \{p\in\Delta(\Om) : p_i\ge p_k\,\,\forall i\in I,\,\forall k\in\{0,1,2\}\}.
\]
($K_I^-$ uses the identity $s\cdot w_\lambda = 2\sum_i\lambda_i s_i - 1$
to identify rowwise minimizers; $B_I$ similarly identifies the labels
making $w_\lambda$ Bayes-optimal at posterior $p$.)

\begin{lemma}[Cone intersection]\label{lem:cone}
For every nonempty $I\subseteq\{0,1,2\}$, if $\rho$ is a Borel
probability on $\Delta(\Om)$ with $\rho(K_I^-) = 1$ and barycenter
$\bar s\in B_I$, then $\rho = \delta_{\mu_0}$ where $\mu_0 = (1/3,
1/3, 1/3)$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}[Sketch]
Fix $i\in I$. $\rho$-a.s., $s_k - s_i \ge 0$ for every $k$. Since
$\bar s\in B_I$, $\bar s_i\ge \bar s_k$, so $\int(s_k - s_i)\,d\rho
\le 0$. A bounded nonnegative Borel random variable with nonpositive
expectation is zero a.s., hence $s_k = s_i$ $\rho$-a.s. for every
$k$. Coordinates summing to one force $s = (1/3, 1/3, 1/3)$
$\rho$-a.s.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[No-free-dust]\label{thm:nofreedust}
Under atomless $\tau$ in the ternary winner-takes-all setting, no
Borel $\tau$-null set $N\subseteq M$, no Borel labeling $w_N: N\to W$,
and no adversarial kernel $\kappa$ supported on rowwise minimizers
can simultaneously satisfy: $q_\beta(N) > 0$ where
$q_\beta = \alpha\tau + (1-\alpha)(\tau\otimes\kappa)_2$, AND
Bayes-cone calibration $q_N$-a.e.\ on $N$ (where $q_N$ is the dust
message marginal).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Sketch]
Define $\nu(ds, dm) := \tau(ds)\,\kappa(dm\mid s)$ on
$\Delta(\Om)\times M$, and let $\nu_N := \nu\!\restriction_{\Delta(\Om)\times N}$.
Disintegrate $\nu_N$ over its second marginal $q_N$: $\nu_N(A\times E) =
\int_E\rho_m(A)\,q_N(dm)$ for Borel kernels $\rho_m$, $q_N$-a.e.\ $m\in N$.
The rowwise-minimizer support gives $\rho_m(K_{I(m)}^-) = 1$ $q_N$-a.e.
The Bayes-cone calibration gives the barycenter of $\rho_m$ in
$B_{I(m)}$. By Lemma~\ref{lem:cone}, $\rho_m = \delta_{\mu_0}$ $q_N$-a.e.
Hence
\[
  \nu(\{\mu_0\}\times N) \;=\; \int_N\rho_m(\{\mu_0\})\,q_N(dm) \;=\; q_N(N).
\]
Now $\tau(N) = 0$, so $q_\beta(N) = (1-\alpha)\,q_N(N)$; assumption
$q_\beta(N) > 0$ gives $q_N(N) > 0$ (assuming $\alpha < 1$; $\alpha = 1$
rules out adversarial dust trivially). On the other hand, $\nu$ has
first marginal $\tau$, so
$\nu(\{\mu_0\}\times N) \le \nu(\{\mu_0\}\times\Delta(\Om)) = \tau(\{\mu_0\}) = 0$
(atomlessness). Contradiction.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{What this buys.} Lemma~\ref{lem:cone} strengthens the
sharpness from one boundary point ($I = \{0\}$, $t_0 =
(0.4,0.3,0.3)$, recovering $K_0^- \cap B_0 = \{\mu_0\}$) to a uniform
statement covering all support patterns. Theorem~\ref{thm:nofreedust}
then closes the most plausible escape: an infinite-setting reading of
Definition~2 ($q_\beta$-a.e., \S\ref{sec:setting}) explicitly allows
adversaries to use $\tau$-null messages, and one might hope to repair
calibration by routing extra labels through such ``null-message
dust.'' The proof never counts messages: it works for finite,
countable, or continuum dust. The obstruction is invariant to
deterministic vs.\ mixed kernels, pure vs.\ mixed dust labels, and
atomic vs.\ diffuse dust.

\paragraph{Sharpness for the boundary witness.} Setting $I = \{0\}$
in Lemma~\ref{lem:cone} recovers the pointwise sharpness at
$t_0 = (0.4, 0.3, 0.3)$: the source cone is $K_0^- = \{s : s_0\le s_1,
s_0\le s_2\}$, the Bayes cone for action $a_0$ is $B(t_0) = \{p :
p_0\ge p_1, p_0\ge p_2\}$, and $K_0^- \cap B(t_0) = \{\mu_0\}$. With
atomless $\tau$, no positive source mass concentrates at $\mu_0$.
Theorem~\ref{thm:nofreedust} then shows no null-message dust
construction can repair this. menu-Hall is therefore genuinely
required for Tier~2 in this geometry.

\section{Classification of the witness}\label{sec:classification}

Lemma~\ref{lem:cone} and Theorem~\ref{thm:nofreedust} establish that
menu-Hall is needed inside the menu engine. They do \emph{not}
falsify unrestricted Theorem~2.

\paragraph{The trust region $T$ used in \S\ref{sec:tight} is not
primitive.} Concretely, $T = \{\mu : \mu(0)\le 0.4\}$ contains beliefs
with each plurality label: $(0.4, 0.3, 0.3)\mapsto a_0$, $(0.1, 0.8,
0.1)\mapsto a_1$, $(0.1, 0.1, 0.8)\mapsto a_2$. Any plurality-vertex
continuation on $T$ produces all three pure profiles $v_0, v_1, v_2$,
so the induced effective menu is the full vertex menu. The off-$T$
Bregman/TRS projection chooses an interior point of $T$ whose induced
profile maximizes $m\cdot w$ over the in-$T$ menu, but since the
in-$T$ menu is already the full vertex set, the projection collapses
to ordinary plurality at $m$. Thus every $m\in\Delta(\Om)$, inside or
outside $T$, induces the same Bayes-optimal WTA vertex, identically
to $T = \Delta(\Om)$.

\paragraph{Consequence.} If the full menu $\{v_0, v_1, v_2\}$ is
optimal under some primitive $(\alpha, \tau, \Theta, f, u, A)$, then
the same behavior is representable as $T = \Delta(\Om)$. If the full
menu is not optimal, neither is the halfspace. Either way, the
boundary number $0.4$ and the boundary point $t_0$ are
\emph{representational scenery, not load-bearing beams}. The witness
demonstrates menu-Hall is genuinely needed inside the
$F$-functional optimization (i.e., choosing among compact menus in
$\mathcal K(W)$), but it does \emph{not} certify that every primitive
optimal solution must hit the same obstruction. To recover the
obstruction, the strategy would need to label a $\tau$-null boundary
point by $v_0$ while sourcing it from $K_0^-$, but the same trust
region already contains all three vertices, so the primitive induced
menu is full.

\section{Comparison to the earlier route}\label{sec:comparison}

The earlier route worked in the agent's strategy space with the
Balder weak topology, used Mertens's asymmetric minmax theorem on a
$\tau$-dominated restricted game, and lifted the restricted maximizer
back to the unrestricted game by a Lusin regularization. It required
three additional hypotheses:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=2em]
\item \textbf{(A5-thick)} an endogenous Lusin-thickness condition on
the maximizer's representative;
\item \textbf{(A8c-attain)} attainment of the rowwise infimum at a
measurable selector;
\item \textbf{(TRE-gen-Hall)} a deterministic version of the present
menu-Hall calibration.
\end{itemize}
The comparison to the current route is summarised below.

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lll}
\hline
\textbf{Conclusion} & \textbf{Earlier route} & \textbf{Current route}\\
\hline
$\sigma^*$ optimal & standing $+$ A5-thick & standing alone\\
$\eps$-adversary    & standing $+$ A5-thick & standing alone\\
exact $\beta^*$    & standing $+$ A5-thick $+$ A8c-attain & standing $+$ exact-contact\\
robust rat'lity   & ditto $+$ TRE-gen-Hall (deterministic) & ditto $+$ menu-Hall (set-valued)\\
\hline
\textbf{Engine}    & Balder weak topology on $\Si$, & compactness of $\mathcal K(W)$ in\\
                   & Mertens minmax, Lusin lift     & finite-dim $\R^{|\Om|}$\\
\hline
\textbf{Sharpness} & pointwise ternary witness     & cone intersection (uniform) +\\
                   &                               & no-free-dust theorem\\
\hline
\textbf{Witness} & possible Th.~2 obstruction     & menu-engine artefact, classified\\
                 & (then unclassified)            & not a Th.~2 counterexample\\
\hline
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

The architectural shift is moving from infinite-dimensional strategy
compactness to finite-dimensional payoff-profile compactness. Four
substantive consequences:

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=2em]
\item Tier 1a is now \emph{unconditional}. A5-thick (a Lusin-thickness
clause that fails in perfect-revelation examples) and A8c-attain (a
rowwise-argmin attainment clause that fails on upward-spike rows) are
both eliminated for value optimality and $\eps$-adversaries.
\item Exact-contact is endogenous to the optimal labeling, so it can
be checked from the chosen $w^*$ rather than from a representative
of an abstract maximizer. The sufficient routes (closed image,
closed-graph correspondence, u.h.c.\ Bayes-action) are all
instantiable by primitive choices.
\item Menu-Hall is set-valued, and the support-function form makes
the calibration condition a transparent integrated separation
inequality. This is strictly weaker than deterministic TRE-gen-Hall
(every deterministic $m^*$ kernel is a degenerate $\kappa$).
\item The sharpness package is uniform: Lemma~\ref{lem:cone} covers
all support patterns, and Theorem~\ref{thm:nofreedust} closes
null-message dust escapes that the $q_\beta$-a.e.\ reading of
Definition~2 (\S\ref{sec:setting}) explicitly allows. The witness's
trust region is classified (\S\ref{sec:classification}) as a
menu-engine artefact, not a counterexample to unrestricted
Theorem~2.
\end{enumerate}
