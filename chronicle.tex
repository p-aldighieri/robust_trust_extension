  Robust Trust Theorem~2 infinite extension:\\
  \large A chronicle of attempted routes
}
\author{Pedro Aldighieri}

\begin{abstract}
This is a research log of the routes attempted to extend the
existence direction of Theorem~2 (``Robustly Rationalizable
Solution'') in Dworczak \& Smolin's \emph{Robust Trust} (2026) to
infinite~$M$ and~$\Th$. It documents what was tried, what worked,
what failed, and where the failures share a common structural cause.
The companion document \emph{exposition.pdf} states the resulting
three-tier theorem and its sharpness; this chronicle exists so that
future contributors do not redo failed work and so that the central
remaining open problem is precisely posed. Section~\ref{sec:reny}
records the route based on Phil Reny's restricted-game suggestion in
detail; subsequent sections record the menu-engine pivot and the
post-v8 strategic search.
\end{abstract}

\section{The question and the gap}\label{sec:gap}

The paper's Theorem~2 has two parts. \emph{Optimality direction:} every
robustly rationalizable strategy is optimal. \emph{Existence direction:}
when $M = \suppset(\tau)$ and $\Th$ are finite, a robustly
rationalizable strategy exists. The optimality direction is
finiteness-free; the existence direction uses Sion's minimax on
products of finite-dimensional simplices and the paper notes that
extending Sion's conditions to infinite cheap-talk-like spaces is
technically difficult.

The project asked: can existence be established in infinite~$M$ and
compact metric~$\Th$, under the model's standing Borel/compact
hypotheses? The answer reached over the course of the routes below
is that a clean three-tier conditional theorem can, but the
unrestricted existence direction remains structurally open at a
single named obstacle: a deletion-compatible Hall duality theorem
(see \S\ref{sec:diagnosis}).

\section{The Phil Reny route}\label{sec:reny}

The first sustained attempt followed Phil Reny's suggestion: split
the proof into a value-securing existence step and an attainment
step, restrict the misaligned adviser to $\tau$-dominated kernels in
the first step, then lift the restricted maximizer back to the
unrestricted game via Lusin regularization.

\paragraph{Stage 1 --- restricted-game maximizer.}
Restrict $B$ to the convex set $F$ of $\tau$-dominated densities
$\beta_\varphi(dm\mid s) = \varphi(m\mid s)\tau(dm)$ with
$\varphi$ jointly Borel and normalized. On the restricted game, two
ingredients combine cleanly:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=2em]
\item \textbf{Balder constant-marginal continuity.} For every fixed
$\varphi\in F$, the map $\sigma\mapsto U_F(\sigma,\varphi) :=
U(\beta_\varphi,\sigma)$ is continuous in the Balder weak topology
$T_\lambda$ (Balder 1988, Theorem~2.2), where $\lambda := \tau\otimes\bar f$
with $\bar f := \sum_\omega\mu_0(\omega)f(\cdot\mid\omega)$.
\item \textbf{Compactness of $\Si$ in $T_\lambda$.} Modulo the
$\lambda$-a.e.\ equivalence (Balder quotient), $\Si$ is compact convex
Hausdorff (Balder 1988, Theorem~2.3(a)).
\end{itemize}
Mertens's asymmetric minmax theorem (Mertens 1986, Corollary~B) then
gives a restricted-game maximizer $\sigma^*\in\Si$ with
$\max_\sigma\inf_F U_F = \inf_F\max_\sigma U_F$. Both mixed-strategy
sides collapse to pure by affineness and the standard barycenter
argument.

\paragraph{Stage 2 --- Lusin lift.}
For each $\beta\in B$ and $\eps>0$, smooth $\beta$ to a
$\tau$-dominated $\varphi_\eps\in F$ losing at most $\eps$ at
$\sigma^*$. The smoothing is built shell-by-shell on a Lusin compact
core $K^* = \bigcup_n K_n$ with full $\pi(\cdot\mid\omega)$-measure
for every~$\omega$, where $\hat\sigma^*$ is continuous on each $K_n$
in the Balder stable topology and every relative open in $K_n$ has
positive $\pi(\cdot\mid\omega)$-mass.

\paragraph{Hypotheses required.}
Three hypotheses entered:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=2em]
\item \textbf{(A5-thick)} the Lusin-thickness condition just stated.
The original (A5) demanded $\pi(\cdot\mid\omega)\sim\tau$ for every
$\omega$; (A5-thick) is strictly weaker.
\item \textbf{(A8c-attain)} the rowwise infimum of the misaligned
payoff $\ell_{\sigma^*}(m,s) := \sum_\omega s(\omega) p_\omega(m)$ is
attained at a measurable selector. This converts the value-securing
$\eps$-adversary into an exact $\beta^*$.
\item \textbf{(TRE-gen-Hall)} a deterministic version of the
posterior-calibration condition needed for full robust
rationalizability: the joint coupling
$\gamma_\alpha = \alpha(\mathrm{id},\mathrm{id})_\#\tau + (1-\alpha)(\mathrm{id},m^*)_\#\tau$
disintegrates with posteriors landing in the per-message Bayes cones
$C(m)$ for $q$-a.e.\ $m$, where $m^*$ is the deterministic worst-message map.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Result and limitation.}
This delivered a two-tier conditional theorem (Tier~1: optimal
$\sigma^*$ and adversarial $\beta^*$ with $U(\sigma^*) = U^* =
U(\beta^*,\sigma^*)$; Tier~2: full robust rationalizability under
the third hypothesis). Each of the three added conditions was shown
\emph{tight for the route}: a perfect-revelation finite example
obstructs the Lusin-smoothing mechanism behind A5-thick, an
upward-spike row obstructs A8c-attain, and a ternary non-radial
witness exhibits an explicit Hall violation showing TRE-gen-Hall is
not derivable from the weaker hypotheses.

The route was real, but the engine was heavy. (A5-thick) was
imposed on a representative of a maximizer of an abstract
infinite-dimensional optimization, and (A8c-attain) inherited that
representative-dependence. The witness for (TRE-gen-Hall) hinted at
something more fundamental than a deterministic-vs-randomized issue,
but the route's deterministic kernel architecture made that hard to
disentangle.

\section{The pivot to the menu engine}\label{sec:pivot}

The architectural change came from observing that the misaligned
payoff at fixed $\sigma$ depends on the agent's strategy only through
its image in the \emph{payoff-profile space}
\[
  W \;:=\; \{w\in\R^{|\Om|} : w(\omega) = \E_{\hat\sigma}[u(a,\omega,\theta)\mid\omega]\text{ for some }\hat\sigma\in\hat\Si\},
\]
which is a compact convex subset of finite-dimensional $\R^{|\Om|}$
(boundedness of $u$ + compact $A$ + continuity of $u$ in $a$). An
agent strategy is a Borel labeling $w: M\to W$, and the value of the
game depends on $w$ only through the compact menu $C := \overline{w(M)}\subseteq W$.
The space $\mathcal K(W)$ of nonempty compact subsets of $W$ is itself
compact under the Hausdorff metric.

This delivered a strictly stronger three-tier theorem (the v8 result,
stated in the companion exposition):
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=2em]
\item \textbf{Tier~1a} (standing alone): existence of value-optimal
$\sigma^*$ and $\eps$-adversaries. \textbf{No added hypotheses.}
\item \textbf{Tier~1b} (+ exact-contact): exact $\beta^*$ via Borel
selector on the rowwise contact set $G(s) := \{m : s\cdot w^*(m) =
\min_{C^\dagger}\,s\cdot z\}$ where $C^\dagger := \overline{w^*(M)}\subseteq C^*$.
\item \textbf{Tier~2} (+ menu-Hall): full robust rationalizability
$q_\beta$-a.e.\ via a set-valued kernel $\kappa$ supported on $G(s)$
with calibrated posterior membership in the Bayes cones $B(m)$.
\end{itemize}

The pivot eliminated A5-thick (Tier~1 became unconditional),
endogenized A8c-attain (now exact-contact, checkable from the chosen
labeling rather than from a representative of an abstract maximizer),
and weakened TRE-gen-Hall to set-valued menu-Hall.

\section{Sharpness}\label{sec:sharpness}

Two strengthenings of the v7 ternary non-radial witness were proved
in v8:

\begin{lemma}[Cone intersection]
For every nonempty $I\subseteq\{0,1,2\}$ and every Borel probability
$\rho$ on $\Delta(\Om)$ supported on $K_I^- := \{s : s_i\le s_k\,\forall i\in I,\,\forall k\}$
with barycenter in $B_I := \{p : p_i\ge p_k\,\forall i\in I,\,\forall k\}$:
$\rho = \delta_{\mu_0}$ where $\mu_0 = (1/3, 1/3, 1/3)$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{theorem}[No-free-dust]
Under atomless $\tau$ in WTA ternary, no Borel $\tau$-null dust set
$N\subseteq M$, no Borel labeling $w_N$, and no adversarial kernel
$\kappa$ supported on rowwise minimizers can simultaneously satisfy
positive $q_\beta(N)$ and Bayes-cone calibration $q_N$-a.e.\ on $N$.
\end{theorem}

The cone intersection lemma upgrades sharpness from a single boundary
point ($I = \{0\}$, $t_0 = (0.4, 0.3, 0.3)$) to a uniform statement
across all support patterns. The no-free-dust theorem closes the
escape that the $q_\beta$-a.e.\ reading of Definition~2 (which allows
adversaries to use $\tau$-null messages) might suggest.

\paragraph{Classification.} Separately, the witness's trust region
$T = \{\mu : \mu(0)\le 0.4\}$ was shown not primitive: its induced
payoff-profile menu is the full vertex set $\{v_0, v_1, v_2\}$,
behaviorally equivalent to $T = \Delta(\Om)$. Hence the witness
exhibits a menu-engine artefact, not a counterexample to unrestricted
Theorem~2.

\section{Post-v8 strategic search: what we tried}\label{sec:routes}

After v8, three further routes were attempted to remove menu-Hall as
an added hypothesis. All three reached defensible terminal endpoints
without succeeding.

\subsection{Strategy~3: canonical/minimal menu route}\label{sec:strategy3}

Idea: enforce a primitive minimality condition on the optimal
labeled menu and ask whether menu-Hall is then derivable. Four
candidate canonicality conditions were evaluated against a
\emph{renaming test}: a candidate is alive only if its primitive
hypothesis does not implicitly require the existence of a kernel on
$G(s)$, posterior membership in $B(m)$, or any direct Bayes-cone
inclusion at messages.
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=2em]
\item[(C1)] \emph{Behavioral minimality.} $C^*$ is $F$-optimal and
no proper compact $D \subsetneq C^*$ is $F$-optimal.
\item[(C2)] \emph{Exposed-extreme canonicality.} $C^*$ is the closed
convex hull of its primitive aligned-best labeling.
\item[(C3)] \emph{Primitive trust-region minimality.} $T^*$ is
minimal among Theorem-1-equivalent trust regions; $C^* = \bigcup_{\mu\in T^*} R(\mu)$
where $R(\mu)$ is the primitive Bayes-profile correspondence.
\item[(C4)] \emph{Radial / orbit symmetry.} A compact group acts
preserving $\tau$, $u$, $W$; the quotient is one-dimensional with a
scalar primitive maximizer.
\end{enumerate}

The critical lemma was \emph{Lemma A.2 (uncalibrated minimal menu
pruning):} under (C1) and (exact-contact), if menu-Hall fails, then
there exists a proper compact $D \subsetneq C^*$ with $F(D) \ge F(C^*)$,
contradicting (C1). The contrapositive form would say: under (C1) +
(exact-contact), menu-Hall holds.

\paragraph{Outcome.} Lemma A.2 stalled at three named structural
gaps under standing + (exact-contact):
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=2em]
\item \emph{Label-saturation gap.} Exact-contact gives nonempty
$G(s)$ but not that touching one $C^*$-minimizer for source $s$
removes all of them.
\item \emph{Replacement-index mismatch.} Hall's separating
certificate uses $z(m)$ indexed by message; deletion improvement
$\ell_D(s) - \ell_{C^*}(s)$ is indexed by source.
\item \emph{Borel-to-compact gap.} $\overline{w^*(M\setminus E)} = C^*$
can hold for $\tau$-positive Borel $E$ (fat-Cantor type example).
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Route~1: deletion-compatible Hall duality (exact theorem)}\label{sec:route1}

After Strategy~3 named the structural gaps, a fresh search identified
the underlying missing object: a duality theorem that prices
sourcewise deletion while enforcing messagewise calibration. The
target theorem was formalized in primitive terms:

\paragraph{Hypothesis (H\textsubscript{del}).} For every Borel
$E\subseteq M$ with $\tau(E)>0$, if $D_E := \overline{w^*(M\setminus E)}$
is a proper compact subset of $C^*$, then $F(D_E) < F(C^*)$.

\paragraph{Conclusion.} There exists a Borel kernel $\kappa: M\to\Delta(M)$
supported on $G(s)$ τ-a.e.\ with the disintegration posterior in
$B(m)$ q-a.e.

A literature search across Strassen/Kellerer, weak optimal transport
(Gozlan-Roberto-Samson-Tetali; Backhoff-Veraguas-Beigl\-böck-Pammer),
martingale OT, capacity-constrained OT, adapted/causal OT, cyclic
monotonicity, and persuasion duality (Doval-Skreta;
Dworczak-Martini; Dworczak-Kolotilin) returned a \emph{Build}
verdict: no off-the-shelf theorem applies under primitive-equivalent
hypotheses. The closest tools provide ingredients but not the
finished bridge.

The natural attack was a finite-partition LP with $\tau$-aligned
mass charged sourcewise, finite Farkas duality, and a collapse lemma
converting infeasibility to a positive-mass deletion certificate. The
LP was attempted twice (representative-label form + sourcewise rewrite).

\paragraph{Outcome.} The first attempt was disproved by a concrete
binary counterexample: representative-label LPs generate phantom
infeasibilities from coarse mixed cells unrelated to any sourcewise
deletion. The sourcewise rewrite cleared that bug but stalled at
four structural walls:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=2em]
\item Continuum-mass label fibers can be $\tau$-null, leaving the
LP's aligned attribution empty.
\item The Borel-to-compact gap survives.
\item Finite $V_0 \subset W$ doesn't control compact max-min over $W$
without uniform Lipschitz mesh.
\item \emph{(H\textsubscript{del}) is pointwise strict, not
uniformly strict.} Proper $D_n \to C^*$ in Hausdorff with
$F(D_n) \uparrow F(C^*)$ are consistent with (H\textsubscript{del}),
so any LP conclusion of the form $F(D_E) \ge F(C^*) - K\eps$ does
not contradict it.
\end{enumerate}

The fourth obstruction is decisive: (H\textsubscript{del}) is too
weak as a primitive hypothesis to generate an LP contradiction.

\subsection{Route~2: calibration-defect (quantitative bound)}\label{sec:route2}

The fourth obstruction in Route~1 motivated a different deliverable:
instead of an exact existence theorem, prove a quantitative bound
\[
  BR(C^*, w^*) \;\le\; \Phi(\Delta_\text{del}(C^*, w^*))
\]
where $BR$ is a primitive payoff-profile Bayes-regret functional and
$\Delta_\text{del}$ is a primitive deletion-defect functional. Under
this framing, a positive defect produces positive regret rather than
requiring contradiction --- the (H\textsubscript{del}) tiny-hinge
obstruction is dodged by quantitative softness.

After three candidate defect functionals were evaluated (the chosen
one, $\Delta_\text{del}^{cp}$, is the compact-patch dual deletion
residual --- primitive, non-tautological), the proof route reduced
to a finite-net entropy-bootstrap with linear $\Phi$ for polytope
$W$ and Hölder $\Phi(\delta) \asymp \delta^{1/(d+1)}$ for curved $W$.

\paragraph{Outcome.} The critical finite-family compact-patch
separation lemma stalled at three obstructions: (i) Borel-to-compact
non-monotonicity --- the signed integrand $s\cdot(v_i - w^*(m))$
makes the inner $\inf_{m\in G(s)}$ non-monotone in $E$, so ordinary
inner regularity does not bridge $E_i$ to compact $K_i$; (ii)
cell-flow lift gap --- LP solutions averaging over source cells
cannot in general lift to Borel kernels supported on $R_0$ without
fiber-thickness or splitability hypotheses outside the standing set;
(iii) slack discipline failure --- the entropy bootstrap requires
$n(\eps)\,\rho_\eps \to 0$ uniformly across the dual class generated
by the finite net, which standing hypotheses don't supply for curved $W$.

The diagnosis: Route~2 found a different scorch pattern. Route~1
died on the pointwise-strict-vs-uniform-strict gap; Route~2 dodges
that wound but the remaining failures are recognizably from the same
family --- Borel certificates do not automatically compactify, and
sourcewise deletion does not automatically lift to messagewise
calibrated fibers.

\section{Convergent diagnosis}\label{sec:diagnosis}

Strategy~3 (Lemma A.2), Route~1 (LP collapse + sourcewise rewrite),
and Route~2 (finite-net forward bound) reach the same locked gate.
The single missing object is what we will call a
\emph{deletion-compatible Hall duality theorem}:
\begin{quote}
Take an optimal labeled menu $(C^*, w^*)$, a rowwise-minimizer
correspondence $G(s) := \{m : s\cdot w^*(m) = \min_{z\in C^*}\,s\cdot z\}$,
and Bayes-optimality cones $B(m) := \{\mu : \hat\sigma^*(m)\in\argmax\,U(\hat\sigma',\mu)\}$.
Give necessary and sufficient conditions \emph{in primitive terms}
(using only $F$, $w^*$, $\tau$, $\alpha$, compact source patches, raw
payoff comparisons $s\cdot v$ for $v\in W$) under which there exists
a Borel kernel $\kappa(\cdot\mid s)$ supported on $G(s)$ τ-a.e.\ such
that the induced disintegration posterior lies in $B(m)$ for q-a.e.~$m$.
\end{quote}
The theorem must price sourcewise deletion (the dual character) and
enforce messagewise calibration (the primal character)
simultaneously, and it must explicitly handle:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=2em]
\item \textbf{Borel-to-compact non-monotonicity.} Signed deletion
integrands prevent compact-patch tests from controlling Borel
violations.
\item \textbf{Label-fiber lift.} Cell-flow LP solutions to Borel
kernels on the contact relation $R_0$.
\item \textbf{Slack discipline.} $n(\eps)\,\rho_\eps \to 0$ uniform
across the dual class generated by an $\eps$-net $V_\eps \subset W$.
\end{itemize}

If such a theorem exists, Strategy~3, Route~1, and Route~2 all
reopen with real force; Tier~2 may become unconditional or replace
menu-Hall by a primitive defect.

\section{What is banked, what is open}\label{sec:status}

\paragraph{Banked.}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=2em]
\item The three-tier theorem (v8 in the companion exposition):
unconditional Tier~1a, exact-contact Tier~1b, set-valued menu-Hall
Tier~2 in the $q_\beta$-a.e.\ reading of Definition~2.
\item The sharpness package: cone intersection lemma + no-free-dust
theorem.
\item The classification of the ternary witness as a menu-engine
artefact, not a counterexample to unrestricted Theorem~2.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Closed routes (not to be revisited under standing alone).}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=2em]
\item The Phil Reny two-stage restricted-game-plus-Lusin-lift route:
superseded by the menu engine, which is strictly stronger.
\item Strategy~3 canonical/minimal menu (under (H\textsubscript{del})
or (C1) alone): blocked at three structural gaps.
\item Route~1 deletion-compatible Hall as an exact theorem under
(H\textsubscript{del}): blocked by pointwise-strict-vs-uniform-strict.
\item Route~2 calibration-defect quantitative bound under
(H\textsubscript{del}) with compact-patch defect: blocked at
Borel-to-compact non-monotonicity, label-fiber lift, slack discipline.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Separate research target.}
A Borel-residual variant of Route~2 (replace the compact-patch
defect with a Borel-patch defect): the natural separation lives in
the Borel world, but the cell-flow lift gap likely still bites and
$\Phi$ degrades. Worth its own pipeline if pursued, not part of this
project's closure.

\paragraph{Open.}
The deletion-compatible Hall duality theorem of \S\ref{sec:diagnosis}.
This is the single object whose existence (or impossibility under
some primitive obstruction) would resolve the unrestricted infinite
extension of Theorem~2 in either direction.
